</objective>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 1: Write Email 1 — Welcome + Vision</name>
  <files>marketing/emails/sequence/01-welcome.md</files>
  <action>
  **Sent:** Immediately after signup
  **Subject:** "You're in. Here's what we're building."
  **From:** Founder name (personal, not "AI Interview Team")

  **Body (~250 words):**
  - Thank them for joining (genuine, not effusive)
  - One paragraph: what AI Interview is and why it matters
  - The key insight: "Every platform added a chatbot to interviews. We're adding Claude Code — a real AI agent that reads files, runs commands, and builds across codebases."
  - What to expect: "I'll send you 2 more emails over the next week — one about the problem we're solving, and one asking for your input on what to build first."
  - Soft CTA: "Hit reply and tell me: does your company allow AI in interviews?"
  - Sign off personally

  Tone: Personal letter from a founder, not a marketing email. No images, no buttons — plain text with maybe one bold line.
  </action>
  <verify>Under 250 words, has reply CTA, feels personal</verify>
  <done>Welcome email drafted and ready to configure</done>
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 2: Write Email 2 — The Problem Deep-Dive</name>
  <files>marketing/emails/sequence/02-problem.md</files>
  <action>
  **Sent:** 3 days after signup
  **Subject:** "The chatbot problem in technical interviews"
  **From:** Founder name

  **Body (~400 words):**
  - Open with the stat: "76% of engineers use AI copilots daily. But interview platforms still give candidates a chat widget."
  - Walk through the problem: existing platforms bolted chatbots onto traditional tests. A chatbot can't read your codebase, can't run commands, can't iterate across files.
  - The Claude Code difference: "It's like testing someone's driving with a steering wheel simulator vs putting them in a real car."
  - Share the METR study insight: experienced devs were 19% slower with AI — proving this is a real, assessable skill
  - Meta's validation: they're rolling AI interviews to all SWE roles in 2026
  - Closing: "This is why we're building AI Interview. Not another chatbot layer. A platform purpose-built for how engineers actually work in 2026."
  - CTA: "Share this with an engineering manager who'd find this interesting: [waitlist URL]"

  Include one or two of the best lines from the blog posts if they're published.
  </action>
  <verify>Under 400 words, provides genuine value, has share CTA</verify>
  <done>Problem email drafted and ready to configure</done>
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 3: Write Email 3 — Survey + Feedback Ask</name>
  <files>marketing/emails/sequence/03-survey.md</files>
  <action>
  **Sent:** 7 days after signup
  **Subject:** "Quick question: what would make you switch?"
  **From:** Founder name

  **Body (~200 words):**
  - "You've been on the waitlist for a week. I want to build this for you, not at you."
  - "Three questions that will shape what we build:" (link to survey from 05-02-PLAN)
  - If they don't want to click the survey, ask the 3 most important questions inline:
    1. "What's your role? (Engineering manager / Recruiter / Engineer / Other)"
    2. "What interview platform do you currently use?"
    3. "What's the #1 thing you'd want in an AI-assisted interview tool?"
  - "Just hit reply with your answers — I read every response."
  - Brief product update: "Here's what we're working on: [one line about progress]"
  - Thank them

  Tone: This is the conversion email — it's how we turn passive interest into validated demand. Make the survey feel effortless.
  </action>
  <verify>Under 200 words, survey link included, reply option as fallback</verify>
  <done>Survey email drafted and ready to configure</done>
</task>
